# Evaluation

What we measured, what we found, and what these numbers do **not** mean.

Raw output: [`ml/evaluation/results/ranking_eval.md`](../ml/evaluation/results/ranking_eval.md)
and `.json`, regenerated by:

```bash
python ml/evaluation/run_eval.py
```

Numbers below are from the run dated `2026-08-19T04:11:40Z` against a 3,075-product
catalog and 82 knowledge-base items across 5 goals. They are measured, not estimated.
If you change the weights in `backend/app/config/ranking.yaml`, rerun before quoting.

---

## What counts as "relevant"

We have **no click logs, no purchase logs and no human relevance judgements.** The
catalog is curated demo data. So we cannot measure user satisfaction, and nothing here
should be read as if we had.

What we *can* measure is **specification conformance.** The knowledge base states, per
requirement, which shelf the item belongs on, which features it must have, and roughly
what it should cost. A product matching all three satisfies the stated need.

| Gain | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 2 | Right shelf, every required feature present, priced within the estimated band (+15% tolerance) |
| 1 | Right shelf, but fails on features or on price |
| 0 | Wrong shelf, or out of stock |

Full rule and its caveats: [`ml/evaluation/relevance.py`](../ml/evaluation/relevance.py).

## Why there are two settings

`candidates_for_requirement` filters to the item's subcategory in SQL, orders the
survivors by TF-IDF, and *only then* hands them to the weighted scorer. Those are two
different jobs. Measuring them in one pool would credit the wrong component.

- **retrieval** — pool is every product in the item's *category*. Which shelf is this?
- **ranking** — pool is every product in the item's *subcategory*, which is what the
  scorer actually receives in production. Shelf is already correct, so the bar rises to
  gain 2: has every required feature and is priced in band.

## Result 1: TF-IDF finds the shelf, not the scorer

| Ranker | P@1 | P@10 | NDCG@10 | MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `semantic_tfidf` | **0.963** | **0.943** | **0.771** | **0.982** |
| `smartbuy` | 0.780 | 0.758 | 0.761 | 0.809 |
| `price_asc` | 0.281 | 0.278 | 0.278 | 0.396 |
| `popularity` | 0.207 | 0.257 | 0.238 | 0.373 |
| `random` | 0.281 | 0.266 | 0.219 | 0.452 |
| `rating` | 0.293 | 0.289 | 0.129 | 0.462 |

**The weighted scorer loses to plain TF-IDF here, and that is the correct outcome.**
Five of its eight components (quality, review strength, delivery, deal value, preference
match) say nothing about whether a product is the right *kind* of thing, so on a
category-wide pool they actively pull irrelevant products upward. This is precisely why
the pipeline applies a hard subcategory filter before scoring rather than asking the
scorer to do retrieval.

The result worth keeping: every non-semantic baseline sits near or below random
(0.219 NDCG@10). Sorting a category by popularity or rating is not a shopping
assistant. `rating` scores *worse* than random because unreviewed 5.0-star products
flood the top.

## Result 2: within the shelf, the scorer wins — by less than it looks

| Ranker | P@1 | P@10 | NDCG@10 | MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `smartbuy` | **1.000** | **0.990** | **0.995** | **1.000** |
| `price_asc` | 0.951 | 0.944 | 0.945 | 0.976 |
| `popularity` | 0.756 | 0.680 | 0.782 | 0.861 |
| `semantic_tfidf` | 0.658 | 0.684 | 0.772 | 0.793 |
| `random` | 0.780 | 0.658 | 0.765 | 0.873 |
| `rating` | 0.110 | 0.365 | 0.519 | 0.306 |

The scorer puts a fully-conforming product at rank 1 for **every one of the 82
queries**. But read the second row before celebrating: sorting by price ascending gets
0.945. And random gets 0.765, because within a single subcategory most products already
satisfy the spec.

**So the honest margin is `smartbuy` over `price_asc`: +0.050 NDCG@10. Not the +0.230
over random.**

## The leak, stated plainly

The scorer's `budget_fit` and `feature_match` components read the same product
attributes the relevance labels are built from. The ranker and the ground truth are not
independent, so **the ranking-setting numbers are an upper bound, not a neutral
measurement.** The ablation shows exactly what that looks like:

| Component | Weight | Δ NDCG@10 (retrieval) | Δ NDCG@10 (ranking) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `budget_fit` | 0.100 | +0.2155 | **+0.2608** |
| `feature_match` | 0.150 | **+0.2156** | +0.0535 |
| `goal_suitability` | 0.250 | +0.0487 | +0.0459 |
| `review_strength` | 0.050 | +0.0381 | +0.0507 |
| `preference_match` | 0.200 | +0.0352 | +0.0484 |
| `quality` | 0.150 | +0.0315 | +0.0441 |
| `delivery` | 0.050 | +0.0265 | +0.0433 |
| `deal_value` | 0.050 | +0.0205 | +0.0408 |

`budget_fit` carries a quarter of the ranking score off a 0.10 weight. That is the leak
being measured, not a discovery about shopping.

**What the leak cannot explain:** `quality`, `review_strength`, `delivery` and
`deal_value` are invisible to the labels — no label consults a rating, a review count, a
delivery time or a discount. Each still contributes +0.04 to +0.05 NDCG@10. That margin
is small, but it is the only part of this evaluation the ground truth could not have
manufactured, and it is the part we would defend.

## What this does not establish

- **Not that the weights are optimal.** We did not search the weight space. The ablation
  shows all eight components have positive marginal value *at the current settings*;
  that is a much weaker claim than optimality, and `ranking.yaml` remains a documented
  prior rather than a fitted model.
- **Not that users prefer these results.** No user was involved at any point.
- **Not real-world retrieval quality.** 3,075 curated products with clean, complete
  attributes are a far easier target than a real marketplace feed.

To make any of these claims we would need human relevance judgements or real interaction
logs, and a held-out split. We have neither, so we do not claim them.

## Reproducing

```bash
python backend/scripts/seed.py     # if the catalog is empty
python ml/evaluation/run_eval.py
```

Metric implementations are unit-tested against hand-worked arithmetic in
[`tests/ml/test_metrics.py`](../tests/ml/test_metrics.py) — the numbers above are only
worth reading if the formulas behind them are right.
